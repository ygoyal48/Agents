Detailed Rationale
Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+’ ratings on the bank facilities of NMDC L
imited
(NMDC)
.
The ratings continue to factor in the strong business risk profile of
NMDC as the largest iron ore producer in the country, high profitability owing to low cost of production, and its strong financial risk and liquidity profiles. The ratings also reflect the strategic importance of the company to the Government of India, also reflected in the majority holding by the government and the company’s Navratna status. This also provides the company preferential treatment under the amended Mines and Miner
al (Development & Regulation) (MMDR) Act. However, business remains susceptible to inherent cyclicality in the steel industry and high working capital requirement due to support extended to select PSU (public sector undertaking) customers through stretched receivables.
Analytical Approach
Crisil Ratings has combined the business and financial risk profiles of NMDC and its subsidiaries, associates and joint ventures. This is because all the entities have common management and strong business and financial
linkages.
Crisil Ratings has also applied its criteria for notching up standalone ratings of entities based on government support. Crisil Ratings believes NMDC will receive support from the government in case of any exigency, considering its strategic importance and majority ownership (60.79%).
Please refer Annexure - List of Entities Consolidated, which captures the list of
entities considered and their analytical treatment of consolidation.
Key Rating Drivers - Strengths
Strong business risk profile as the largest iron ore producer in the country
NMDC is the largest iron ore producer in India, with average annual production capacity of more than 55 million tonne per annum (MTPA). Annual production was ~53 million tonne (MT) in fiscal 2026, against 44 MT in fiscal 2025, whereas annual sales volume was 50 MT and 45 MT, respectively.
NMDC has seven operating iron ore mining leases (five in Chhattisgarh and two in Karnataka), with total reserve of 1,891 MT, providing high revenue visibility with mining life of over three decades. It has environment clearances for all the mines, and long-term validity of licences (till 2035–2038) for six of the seven mines. The licence for the Kumaraswamy mine in Karnataka was renewed in fiscal 2023 and
is valid till October 2042. NMDC plans to increase its production and evacuation capacity, with a targe
t to expand to around 100 MT by fiscal 2030 through brownfield and greenfield projects. It has recently got environmental clearance for Deposit 4 mines in Chhattisgarh under the joint venture, NMDC-CMDC Ltd, which will increase the overall capacity from 55 MTPA to more than 60 MTPA over the medium term.
The company has also expanded into coal — having two coal fields — one operational and the other is expected to be operational over the medium term.
Healthy operating profitability
NMDC had healthy opera
ting margin of 50–60% over fiscals 2018–2021, supported by its stable and low-cost mining operations.
However, as per the amended MMDR Act, 2021, NMDC has been paying a premium of 22.5% of the average selling price (in addition to existing iron ore royalty, DMF, NMEDT payout of 20%) on all its mines from fiscal 2022. Hence, earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortisation (Ebitda) margin started to moderate since fiscal 2022.
Nonetheless, NMDC continues to be the lowest cost producer globally
with cost of production (excluding statutory taxes and levies) of around Rs 1,000 per tonne. The company earns higher realisation on per tonne of iron ore, compared with industry average, owing to better ore quality with iron content of 63–69%, also among the highest grades. Thus, low cost of production and healthy realisation support high operating profitability and strong cash accrual. Despite moderating in the first nine months of fiscal 2026, prices have been increasing since January 2026 due to high d
emand of high grade iron ore, imposition of safeguard duty and geo political uncertainities.
Overall, margin per tonne in fiscal 2026 is expected to moderate due marginal decline in prices and increase in fixed cost per tonne in the first nine months of the fiscal. While absolute margin is expected to be supported by increase in iron ore production or sales, with rise in the share of sale of pellets and HR coils.
Contribution from coal mines is expected to come from this fiscal onwards given Tokisud mine
s had commissioned in January 2026 and is expected to ramp up production by end of this fiscal contributing to the overall Ebitda of the company.
Majority ownership by, and significant strategic importance to, the Government of India, resulting in low regulatory risk
NMDC is majority held by the Government of India (60.79%) and under the administrative control of the Ministry of Steel. High strategic importance to the government is reflected in the Navratna status and leading position of the company in t
he domestic iron ore industry as iron ore is a key raw material for steel production. Being a public sector enterprise (PSE), NMDC receives preferential treatment under the amended MMDR Act, 2021. The act permits special powers to the government to allocate mines and renew mining licences of PSEs.
Strong financial risk profile supported by net cash position
Capital structure and debt protection metrics are healthy, aided by strong networth, absence of significant long-term debt and healthy operating cash
accrual and cash balance, resulting in net cash position over the years. Gearing and total outside liabilities to tangible networth ratio for fiscal 2026 is expected to be 0.12–0.13 time (0.12 time in fiscal 2025) and 0.30–0.35 time (0.27 time in March 2025) and are expected below 0.1 time and 0.30 time, respectively, over the medium term. Furthermore, while NMDC is in the process of increasing its capacity, Crisil Ratings understands that the capital expenditure (capex) undertaken for it will be funded lar
gely through internal accrual. Resultantly, NMDC is expected to continue maintaining its strong financial risk profile over the medium term.
Key Rating Drivers -

knesses
Susceptibility to inherent cyclicality in the steel sector
Iron ore is the key raw material for production of steel, which is an inherently cyclical industry. NMDC sells around 70% of its iron ore to three counterparties — Rashtriya Ispat Nigam Ltd (RINL), JSW Steel Ltd and Arcelormittal Nippon Steel India Pvt Ltd (erstwhile Essar Steel Ltd; rated ‘Crisil AA+/Stable/Crisil A1+’). This makes the company vulnerable to decline in demand or realisation during a downturn in the steel industry and could impact the volume and operating cash flow. However, low cost of production and high ore quality offer some cushion against the offtake risk.
High working capital requirement due to support extended to select PSU customers through stretched receivables
The short-term debt had increased due to rise in receivables from NMDC Steel Ltd (NSL) and RINL since fiscal 2025. While NMDC has been using bill discounting facilities against the increased receivables, it is still reflected as debt under NMDC liabilities given the facilities are with recourse. However, with NSL achieving stabilisation, receivables are expected to moderate gradually. Also, for RINL, while receivables remain elevated, cost of financing is recovered from the debtor itself. While NMDC has tak
en steps to balance support to its customers and managing financing costs, further increase in the receivables will remain monitorable.
Liquidity
Superior
Liquidity is supported by healthy cash accrual (despite significant dividend payout over the years), low bank limit utilisation and no major term debt obligation. Cash accrual was around Rs 4,500 crore in fiscal 2025 against nil term debt obligation. Healthy annual cash accrual of Rs 6,000–7,000 crore is likely to support the yearly capex of Rs 6,000–7
,000 crore that is being undertaken for capacity expansion. As on September 30, 2025, the cash balance stood at ~Rs 12,000 crore.
ESG
P
rofile
Crisil Ratings believes the environment, social and governance (ESG) profile of NMDC supports its already strong credit risk profile.
The mining sector has significant impact on the environment owing to higher emissions, waste generation and water consumption. This is because of the energy-intensive mining process and conversion of iron ore into pellets, which has
high dependance on natural resources such as coking coal and iron ore (key inputs). The sector’s social impact includes the nature of its operations, affecting the local community and the health hazards involved.
Key ESG highlights:
NMDC aims to achieve net zero emissions by 2040
In fiscal 2025, intensity of scope 1 and scope 2 was at 0.0042 tCO2e per tonne similar to fiscal 2024. The company is actively integrating green energy solutions, including wind power plants with a total capacity of 10.5 megawatt (MW) and solar power systems across various project sites, which currently generates approximately 19 million units of wind power and sources 1.64 million units of solar energy, accounting for ~1.4% of total electricity consumption
NMDC achieved an 18% reduction in overall Ozone-depleting substances (ODS) emissions compared to fiscal 2024
The company's project sites operate with a zero liquid discharge system, ensuring that no water is released from the project areas. Effluent water is treated and repurposed for horticultural use through effluent treatment plants (ETPs) and sewage treatment plants (STPs)
NMDC reported a significant improvement in its lost time injury frequency rate (LTIFR) among workers, which decreased to 0.17 in fi
scal 2025 from 0.38 in the previous fiscal, with zero fatalities reported
Its governance structure is characterised by ~50% independent directors in the board, dedicated ESG committee, and adequate financial and non-financial disclosure
Outlook
Stable
Crisil Ratings believes NMDC will maintain its leading market position in the domestic iron industry and continue to benefit from its low cost of production, resulting in healthy cash accrual.

About the Company
NMDC, incorporated in 1958, is a Navaratna PSE, primarily involved in iron ore mining. It is under the administrative control of the Ministry of Steel, Government of India. It has seven operational iron ore mining leases — five in Chhattisgarh and two in Karnataka. NMDC is listed on the Bombay Stock Exchange and the National Stock Exchange. Its market capitalisation stood at Rs 78,256 crore as on May 4, 2026.
